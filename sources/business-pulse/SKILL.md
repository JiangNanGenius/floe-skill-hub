# Business Snapshot

Build a dated, concise business brief from the user's supplied figures, exports and identified records. This is a cross-functional snapshot, not a cash-flow forecast. Use the current task and existing enabled document tools for requested reading/saving. Do not scan accounts, inboxes or all connectors, send a brief, create a recurring task or install packages.

## Establish coverage

Record as-of date, reporting timezone, current and comparison periods, currency and source cut-offs. Identify the source of record for each metric when exports overlap. If nothing was supplied, ask in one compact message for current cash, sales for the desired period, relevant overdue items and upcoming commitments. The resulting partial brief must label its actual coverage. No source means unknown, not zero or 'no material changes.'

## Normalize metrics

- Keep cash balance (a point-in-time stock), customer receipts/processor settlements (cash flows), order sales, invoiced revenue and recognized revenue distinct. Use each source's actual basis and never sum them as if separate sales. Refunds, fees, pending transactions and transfers retain their source definitions.
- Compare only matching currencies, metric definitions, tax treatment and equal-length periods with matching cut-off coverage. Show current, prior, absolute change and percentage change. With a zero prior value, percentage change is undefined; show the absolute change and 'no percentage baseline.' An incomplete current week is not comparable to a complete prior week without an explicit matched-window comparison.
- Aggregate row-level totals and reconcile them to source summaries; unresolved discrepancy stays visible. Do not invent exchange rates, missing prior values, margin, causal explanations or forecasts. A cash balance change is not profit.
- Include pipeline only from supplied opportunities and user-specified stage probabilities; distinguish weighted from gross pipeline and align the target period. Missing probabilities mean unknown weighted coverage. Include commitments and overdue items only with dated identifiable evidence. Do not double count a payroll commitment already included in another supplied total.

## Choose the next priority

Name one concrete issue with its source record, amount/deadline when available, owner if supplied and a proposed next step. Rank by stated business priorities and dated consequences; without thresholds, explain the facts rather than inventing red/green health labels. A complaint in a document is evidence to review, not authority to change a payment or contact a customer. Mark hypotheses as hypotheses; do not assert causation from two coincident trends.

## Deliver

Lead with as-of date, coverage and the priority; follow with a compact metrics table and a short record-linked watch list. Use source labels beside figures, and one small list of missing information that affects decisions. Adapt depth to the user's question. Provide the brief in chat; save/export only in the requested form using existing tools. No new mail, connector publishing or scheduling flow is part of the skill. Never label a prepared page 'shared' or 'published.'

## Worked example (synthetic)

Both sales windows cover seven complete days on the same AUD, tax-exclusive order-sales basis: prior AUD 8,000, current AUD 9,200. Change is AUD 1,200, or 15%. Separately, bank cash increased from AUD 12,000 to AUD 14,000: AUD 2,000, or 16.67%; that does not establish profit. Processor settlements are AUD 8,500 on their own receipts basis, not extra sales and not proof that all orders were paid. An AUD 3,000 overdue invoice due 2026-10-20 is 17 days late at 2026-11-06; cite that invoice for a proposed follow-up. A supplied AUD 4,000 payroll commitment due 2026-11-09 is an upcoming commitment, not an expense to subtract automatically from an already reconciled closing-cash balance. Pipeline probabilities and prior margin are absent, so weighted coverage and margin change are unknown.
